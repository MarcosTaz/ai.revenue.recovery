# Aviation Recovery — Assisted Pilot Scope of Work

## 1. Purpose

Identify, validate and prepare recovery of contractually available aircraft-lease entitlements that may have been missed, under-claimed, delayed or incompletely evidenced.

The initial pilot focuses on maintenance-reserve reimbursements and lessor maintenance contributions. Cost-sharing, security-deposit/prepaid-rent releases and end-of-lease adjustments may be included only when explicitly placed in scope.

## 2. Recommended pilot scope

A standard pilot covers:

- 3–10 leased aircraft and/or engines;
- 1–3 entitlement families;
- 12–24 months of maintenance events or another agreed review window;
- governing lease/amendment package;
- reserve/contribution statements;
- relevant MRO invoices/workscopes and completion evidence;
- prior claim/settlement records;
- scoped finance extracts required to reconcile the selected events.

## 3. Deliverables

### A. Recovery Scan

- entitlement inventory;
- candidate register;
- claim deadline/time-bar map;
- evidence-gap register;
- prioritized high/medium/low recovery opportunities.

### B. Forensic Reconciliation

For each selected candidate:

- governing lease/rule version;
- deterministic calculation;
- eligible-cost build-up;
- reserve/contribution balance reconciliation;
- prior claim/payment offsets;
- exclusions, caps, floors and deductibles;
- red-team result;
- evidence lineage.

### C. Claim-Ready Dossier

For candidates that pass the economic and evidence gates:

- claim summary;
- contractual basis;
- calculation schedule;
- source/evidence references;
- external claim package suitable for customer review/submission.

### D. Executive Closeout

Report separately:

- verified settled recovery, if settlement occurs within the engagement;
- claim-ready economic value;
- review-required pipeline;
- blocked potential and reasons;
- upcoming deadlines and next actions.

## 4. Customer responsibilities

Customer provides:

- authorized access to in-scope contract and evidence documents;
- a business owner able to resolve lease/maintenance/finance questions;
- confirmation of material contract interpretations where language is ambiguous;
- review and approval before any claim package is sent externally;
- settlement/payment evidence required to verify recovered value.

## 5. Aviation Recovery responsibilities

Aviation Recovery will:

- preserve source lineage for material inputs;
- make unknown or missing information explicit rather than infer favorable terms;
- reconcile known prior claims and settlements to reduce duplicate recovery risk;
- red-team positive candidates before claim-ready classification;
- maintain separation between identified, claim-ready, submitted, approved and settled value;
- treat customer confidential data according to the agreed handling requirements.

## 6. Exclusions unless separately agreed

- legal opinions or representation as external counsel;
- tax advice;
- aircraft airworthiness determinations;
- regulatory compliance certification;
- negotiation authority with the lessor/counterparty;
- audit opinion under an accounting/auditing standard;
- ERP/M&E integration program;
- whole-fleet transformation beyond the agreed pilot scope.

## 7. Commercial structure

Indicative assisted-pilot options:

### Recovery Scan

**€7,500–€15,000** depending on asset count, document volume and contract complexity.

### Forensic Reconciliation / Claim Dossier Workstream

**€15,000–€50,000+** depending on number/complexity of claims and evidence reconstruction required.

### Optional success component

Where legally and commercially appropriate, an agreed percentage may apply to **verified recovered value**. Verified recovered value means economic value supported by settlement evidence such as cash receipt, credit memo, refund or contractually valid offset — not merely identified, submitted or approved value.

### Ongoing Recovery Monitoring

Indicative **€2,500–€10,000+ per month** according to fleet size, entitlement scope and service depth.

Final pricing and success-fee structure are agreed in the signed engagement terms.

## 8. Acceptance standard

A pilot is accepted when:

1. every reviewed candidate has an explicit status;
2. every material calculation input is traceable to a source or recorded as a gap;
3. prior claims/payments within the supplied scope are reconciled;
4. deadlines are explicit where determinable;
5. false positives are rejected with recorded reasons;
6. claim-ready outputs pass both economic and evidence-lineage gates;
7. the executive report does not present blocked/submitted/approved value as settled recovery.

The pilot can be successful even when no claim is ultimately recoverable if the work provides evidence-based assurance that the scoped entitlement population has been reconciled and explains why candidates fail.

## 9. Scale decision

At closeout the customer can choose to:

- stop after the bounded pilot;
- expand to additional leases/assets/events;
- add other entitlement families;
- move to recurring recovery monitoring;
- scope integrations and enterprise controls for a broader deployment.
