## Score interpretation

- 90–100: evidence-backed repeatable company; requires real paid outcomes.
- 80–89: build/pilot aggressively while closing material evidence gaps.
- 70–79: narrow wedge or distribution until stronger evidence appears.
- <70: reject, merge or materially reframe.

GovMargin is therefore **NARROWED BUILD / PILOT-PREP**, not SCALE.

## What changed

The downgrade is not because the underlying economics failed. It is because competitive evidence became stronger:

- Costpoint Schedule I explicitly supports cumulative direct/indirect claimed-and-billed reporting and can identify contracts requiring billing adjustment from over/under-billing.
- Costpoint's Final Billing Report includes contract/funded value, total billed, final invoice and payment status.
- Specialist GovCon accounting firms already perform indirect-rate, billing, incurred-cost and closeout work.

The surviving hypothesis is not "software notices underbilling." It is:

**independent multi-system forensic reconstruction that is useful when historical truth is fragmented across ERPs, migrations, manual invoices, rate agreements, bank/payment evidence and acquired entities—and that reduces qualified-review effort through deterministic lineage and fail-closed controls.**

## Score-up evidence

Raise the score only after evidence such as:

1. three independent contractors complete historical reconciliation;
2. at least two pilots find legitimate incremental value that their normal ERP/accountant process had not already surfaced, or an equivalent high-value overbilling-prevention result;
3. at least one supported contractor-side balance is validly billed/true-d up and collected;
4. a qualified legal/accounting review confirms the operating and compensation model;
5. a partner channel provides repeatable data access/referrals;
6. measured audit gross margin and time-to-first-finding support pricing;
7. a cross-system/migration case proves material value specifically outside the active ERP's ordinary reporting.

## Score-down / kill evidence

Lower or reject the thesis if:

1. representative pilots show almost no legitimate incremental value after allowability, funding, ceilings and prior billing;
2. Costpoint/Unanet/other incumbent workflows plus normal GovCon accounting review already surface substantially all target variances at lower cost;
3. data reconstruction cost routinely exceeds likely customer value;
4. customer procurement/sales cycles make low-capital acquisition uneconomic;
5. professional-practice or claims-representation constraints remove the viable operating model;
6. false-positive rates cannot remain extremely low under messy real contractor data;
7. cross-system independence does not create measurable willingness to pay.
