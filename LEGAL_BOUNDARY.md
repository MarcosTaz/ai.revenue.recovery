# GovMargin legal / professional boundary — launch posture

## Decision

**Launch with fixed-fee audit / monitoring pricing. Success-linked compensation remains disabled unless qualified U.S. counsel and GovCon accounting review clear the specific structure.**

This is a product/commercial safety rule, not a legal opinion.

## Federal contingent-fee rule is not a blanket post-award recovery ban

FAR Subpart 3.4 and FAR 52.203-5 define and restrict contingent fees tied to **soliciting or obtaining a Government contract**. GovMargin's historical reconciliation service is post-award, so that rule alone does not establish that a recovery-linked fee is prohibited or permitted.

Source:
- https://www.acquisition.gov/far/subpart-3.4
- https://www.acquisition.gov/far/52.203-5

Operational consequence: do not cite FAR 3.4 as affirmative permission for a success fee.

## Consultant-cost allowability creates a separate economic issue

FAR 31.205-33 addresses professional and consultant service costs. It states that such costs are generally allowable subject to the rule's requirements when reasonable and **not contingent upon recovery of the costs from the Government**.

Source:
- https://www.acquisition.gov/far/31.205-33

Operational consequence:
- GovMargin must never promise that its own fee is an allowable reimbursable contract cost.
- A customer must not automatically allocate a recovery-contingent GovMargin fee to a Government contract.
- Any accounting treatment of GovMargin fees is the customer's qualified accounting determination.

## Anti-Assignment Act boundary

31 U.S.C. § 3727 defines an assignment to include transfer of a claim or an interest in a claim and generally restricts assignment before a claim is allowed, its amount decided and a warrant issued, subject to statutory exceptions.

Source:
- https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title31-section3727

Operational consequence:
- GovMargin must not take ownership of, an assignment of, or direct payment rights in a contractor's Government claim/receivable without qualified legal review.
- Commercial invoices should be ordinary fees owed by the contractor, not an assignment of Government payment rights.

## Representation and reserved-practice boundary

GovMargin's software/analytical service may:

- organize source documents;
- reconstruct deterministic contract economics;
- reconcile earned / billed / paid amounts;
- identify evidence gaps;
- create a finance-review schedule;
- flag accounting or legal issues for qualified review.

GovMargin does not by default:

- render legal opinions;
- certify Contract Disputes Act claims;
- represent a corporation in litigation;
- provide a CPA audit/attestation opinion;
- make unsupported allowability/allocability judgments;
- communicate a disputed legal entitlement to the Government as counsel or authorized professional representative.

Specific representation before agencies, boards or courts must be separately checked against the applicable forum, professional-practice and authorization rules.

## False-claims / overbilling posture

Commercial incentives must never suppress an unfavorable result. A suspected overbilling, refund, rebate, credit or other amount potentially owed to the Government remains visible and review-gated.

GovMargin therefore separates:

- supported recovery candidate;
- payable / overbilling candidate;
- no variance;
- accounting review;
- legal review;
- insufficient evidence / unknown.

## Pricing posture

### Enabled for pilot discovery

- fixed-fee design-partner audit;
- fixed-fee larger forensic audit;
- fixed monthly/annual monitoring subscription;
- fixed portfolio / partner pricing.

### Disabled pending qualified review

- percentage of Government recovery;
- ownership/assignment of a Government claim or receivable;
- fee payable directly from Government proceeds under an assignment structure;
- representations that GovMargin's fee itself is an allowable Government-contract cost;
- legal-claim representation bundled into the software fee.

## Required review before any success-linked model

Obtain a written qualified review covering at least:

1. applicable state law / professional-practice rules for the contracting entity and engagement;
2. whether the scope constitutes legal or reserved accounting practice;
3. any agency/forum representation rules;
4. 31 U.S.C. § 3727 Anti-Assignment Act implications;
5. FAR 31.205-33 treatment of professional/consultant costs;
6. conflicts created by a fee that rewards only favorable findings;
7. safeguards preserving mandatory disclosure of payables/overbilling;
8. customer contract language making clear that GovMargin does not own the Government receivable.

Until that review is completed, the product configuration must expose **no success-fee option**.
